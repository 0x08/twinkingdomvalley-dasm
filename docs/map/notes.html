A small label at the end of a line, eg NE or S+up, gives the direction to type when the line doesn't already point that way. Short arrows lead off to the other panels.

Things the game does itself

  • The fissure and the canyon. Going over the edge sends you diving down a dangerous fall (33), to land below where you jumped: {FALLS}.
  • The Crystal Bridge (97). Wave the short wooden rod at either edge of the fissure (19 or 23) and the way across leads onto the bridge instead of over the edge; wave it again and it's gone.
  • The waterfall. North from the top of the waterfall is a dive (37) into the crocodile pool (1A), and you scramble out by the large rock (1B).
  • The moat (32). Every way towards the castle except the drawbridge goes into the moat, and you scramble out where you came from. Once you have crossed the drawbridge, it closes when you come back out to the desert by the castle (28), unless you carry the uniform, and north from there leads into the moat too.
  • The magic pool at Watersmeet (89). Drink from it to learn the secret of concealed doors: seven secret doors (purple) appear.
  • The rock fall in the small cave (93). The keeper of the mountains lets you east to the river of gold only when you bring the desert king's treasure chest.
  • The Forest King (6D). His guard arrest you (A9) until you pay. A gift worth enough frees you, with an amulet; anything less and you are thrown into the room full of bones. Gifts go to the strong room (B0). Free the princess (A8) wearing the amulet and the king gives you the silver key.
  • The end of your life (AF) is where you go when you die.

Quirks in the game's own data

  • 76 of the exits are one way. Besides the mazes: the quarry (07) leads only to the road, though the scree and the rocks lead into it; the ways onto the high mountains at 11 from 12, 14, 31, 99, AB and AD can't be walked back; nor can the desert's paths up to the mountains at 30 (from 2A and 2F), or the corridor at 59 going SE to 5D.
  • 20 pairs of exits don't reverse each other, eg the small bedroom's SE leads to the small kitchen, but the way back is SW.
  • The high mountains at 30 and 31 both have a way north that leads back to the same place.

Checked against the Electron User maps (1985)

Every box on the five magazine maps matches a place in the game, and almost every line, door, key and object on them does too. The differences:

  • Map 1. It joins the scree slope (06) straight to the stream (09); in the game both lead into the woods (0A) between them. It draws the quarry as two-way to the scree and the rocks; the quarry's only way out is the road. Missing: SE from the small stream (08) to the spring (03), and N from the road (99) to the high mountains (11).
  • Map 2. It draws the high mountains at 14 twice, east of the waterfall and south of AB, without joining them; in the game it's one place, which the waterfall leads into one way. Missing: the path between the dense forest (0F) and the woods (0E). Its "Gold" sits by the crocodile pool; the gold is in the high mountains (31), as map 3 says.
  • Map 3. Missing: the one-way mountain paths from the desert (2F) and east of the castle (2A) up to the high mountains (30).
  • Map 4. Its sloping maze shows shortest routes only, but it joins two rooms (90 and 92) that aren't joined: east from 90 leads to 8F. The "Brass" door from the cave (35) is a metal door that the bronze key opens too. The strong room (B0) starts empty, not with gold: it's where the Forest King puts your gift. The diamond ring starts with the dwarf in the small chamber (6B), not on the spiral staircase. Both twisting maze routes are right.
  • Map 5. There is no silver door between the corridor (5A) and the east tower (55); upstairs, the only silver door is to the west tower (52). The door from the large hall (48) to the dungeon (49) is brass, not bronze. The secret door from the royal bedroom (5B) is one way.
